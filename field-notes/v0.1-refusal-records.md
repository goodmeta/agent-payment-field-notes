# When an agent payment is refused, what can the owner see?

**Field notes v0.1**  
**Eric Tsang, Good Meta**  
**Version date:** 2026-11-24  
**Test window:** 2026-11-10 to 2026-11-17  
**Vendor responses included through:** 2026-11-20

Agent-payment platforms let an owner place limits on what an agent may spend. This note looks at the record after those controls act: when a payment or paid call is refused, or a checkout changes after buyer approval, can the owner later see what happened and which rule applied?

I ran hands-on tests on Locus, Sapiom, Catena and Crossmint between November 10 and November 17, 2026. These are field notes, not a benchmark or ranking. The products differ, the scenarios are not exhaustive, and the results describe only the tested environments and dates.

## Method

The common questions were:

1. Does the control actually stop the attempted action?
2. Does the refusal or changed state leave a record?
3. Can the owner find that record through the product's API or console?
4. Does the record show the reason and the limit or approval state that applied, rather than only current settings?
5. Can the owner enumerate relevant events without already knowing every internal user or agent identifier?

I used sandbox environments where available. Catena tests used test USDC on Base Sepolia. Crossmint Agent Checkouts uses production keys on real merchants, so those tests stopped before payment and bought nothing. Locus tests used a Free workspace and small paid control calls. Sapiom tests used its sandbox and did not reach money movement.

Each finding below is limited to the named scenario. A missing record in the tested API and console views does not prove that no internal event exists anywhere. See the [methodology](../methodology.md) for evidence and exclusion rules.

## Locus Pro: enforcement held; two refusal types were absent from call history

Tested November 11, 2026. The paid resource was `openweather/current-weather` at a fixed 3.6 credits. Call history was read with `GET /api/credits/calls?status=all`, using both the secret key and agent-key views.

| Scenario | Observed result | In call history? |
|---|---|---|
| Agent-key per-call cap of 2 credits below the 3.6-credit price | `403 agent_connection_call_limit_exceeded` | No |
| Per-call price ceiling of 2 credits | `409 charge_ceiling_exceeded` | No |
| Agent-key per-loop budget | `403 AGENT_LOOP_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` | Yes, as `status: error` |
| Workspace monthly hard limit | `403 TENANT_MONTHLY_SPEND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` | Yes, as `status: error` |
| No limiting rule, control call | `200`, charged 3.6 credits | Yes, as `status: charged` |

The two missing refusal types were repeated. Both returned without `X-Locus-Api-Call-Id`. The keys page still showed the capped agent key as recently used, so some system had observed the attempt even though the tested owner views did not list it.

A concurrency check was a positive result: three simultaneous calls sharing a per-loop budget that could fit only one resulted in exactly one charged call and two refusals. A later round mostly encountered the Free plan's rate limit, so that second round is not evidence about budget enforcement.

Past call records also did not identify which workspace limit was in force. A call made with a $3 monthly limit and another made after changing it to $4 had the same record shape; the spend-controls endpoint returned current values, not the historical value.

**Scope limits:** only the Free plan was tested. Enterprise webhooks, ledger features and policy-event endpoints may behave differently. Activity pages or exports not inspected could contain additional information. Skipping these records may be intentional; the documentation says nothing is sent or charged when a ceiling refuses a call.

Detailed note and reproduction script: [Ectsang/locus-limit-record](https://github.com/Ectsang/locus-limit-record).

## Sapiom: the latest checkout state did not preserve what the buyer approved

Tested in the Sapiom sandbox on November 10, 2026.

A buyer confirmed an $11.00 purchase using the card's security code. After later checkout updates, the same transaction read `authorized` at $14.00 and then $18.00, each with a new `authorizedAt`. No buyer step was captured at either changed total. The transaction's latest state showed $18.00; the $11 approval was no longer visible in that state.

The store refused the item, no payment was created at the changed totals, and the test did not reach money movement. **This is a finding about the record, not evidence that Sapiom charged the buyer $14 or $18.**

A separate tool, `purchase-receipts`, captured the sandbox reads as signed, hash-chained receipts. Replaying those reads identified the authorized revisions without a captured buyer step. The tool proves the integrity of the captured buyer-side records, not the truth of the provider's statements; provider-side revision records would be stronger.

The test also could not read the applicable spending-limit versions with the sandbox platform key: the limits endpoint returned `403 Missing required permissions: org.read`. This may be a permissions or setup issue and is not presented as an enforcement failure.

Detailed note, receipt format and verifier: [Ectsang/purchase-receipts](https://github.com/Ectsang/purchase-receipts).

## Catena: strong policy records, but pre-intent refusals were invisible

Tested in Catena's sandbox on November 17, 2026.

### What worked

- A $1 control payment completed.
- A funded $4 payment against a $3 per-transaction cap created an intent with `blocked/blocked`, the rule, and the threshold.
- A $2.50 payment requiring approval was denied in the console and retained the denial trail.
- After the cap changed from $3 to $5, the earlier blocked intent continued to show the $3 threshold. The policy change itself was also logged.
- The activity feed and Agent money activity report showed the completed, blocked and denied payments.

### The two gaps

**Insufficient funds.** A $1 send with a zero balance returned `422 movement_insufficient_funds` and created no intent. The attempted payment did not appear in the inspected activity or report views. Catena's documentation says "No intent" for this case, so the absence of an intent was documented behavior.

**Funds check before policy check.** With the account underfunded, a $4 send against a $3 cap again returned `movement_insufficient_funds`. No intent or cap-breach record was created. The agent's attempt to exceed its owner-set limit was therefore invisible in the tested owner views.

These results do not imply that policy blocks generally go unrecorded. The funded policy-block and denial controls were recorded well. The gap concerns attempts refused before an intent exists.

### Catena's response

On November 19, Catena confirmed that both points were correct. It identified owner visibility into agent attempts as the underlying issue, agreed that an insufficient-balance attempt should be visible to the owner despite the documented 422 behavior, and said Catena was working on an improvement.

The finding remains open until the same scenarios are rerun. The reported improvement is not claimed to work until it has been retested. A future note should distinguish the original November 17 behavior from the tested updated behavior.

## Crossmint: clear per-run refusal records; project-wide list confirmed absent as of November 20

Tested November 17, 2026, using Agent Checkouts with production keys but no purchase.

### What worked

A checkout for a $14 item with a `constraints.maxCost` of $0.01 ended as `blocked` with:

```text
result.code: policy.max_cost_exceeded
```

The result included a plain-English summary naming the item price and cap, and the API record retained the applicable constraints. A second test using a €0.01 cap at a USD merchant was also blocked. That observed behavior was safer than the documentation's cross-currency wording suggested, although an external test cannot establish the internal mechanism responsible.

An earlier run whose task explicitly said "Do not buy" ended without a purchase and never evaluated the cap. It was confounded by that instruction and is excluded as a finding.

### The visibility gap

`GET /unstable/agent-checkouts?limit=10` returned the test user's runs, including status, result and constraints. Supplying another `x-crossmint-user-id` returned an empty list. The console had no Agent Checkouts view, and Orders showed nothing.

A business owner therefore needed to retain every end-user ID and query each user separately to build a consolidated refusal view. The tested record was useful; project-wide discovery of those records was the gap.

### Crossmint's response

On November 18, Crossmint's technical lead for agent products said that they could not point to a project-wide view and were checking with the product team whether one existed or was planned. This was not yet final product-team confirmation.

The technical lead asked whether a project-wide API list would cover the business owner's need. I replied that it would and that a console was not necessary for this finding. The requested API properties were:

- Enumerate runs across end users without knowing every user ID in advance.
- Include blocked runs.
- Return user and run IDs, timestamp, status, result code and summary, and the applicable constraint.
- Support pagination and filtering by status or user.

I offered to rerun the November 17 tests and update this note if such an endpoint is exposed.

On November 20, a Crossmint support agent returned on the same thread and confirmed that **no project-wide list of checkouts existed as of that date, in either the API or console**. Listing checkouts returned only the runs belonging to the supplied `x-crossmint-user-id`, so an owner had to retain every user ID to see every refusal.

Crossmint passed the requested fields to its product team as a feature request, including pagination and filters by status and user. There was no ship date. Crossmint expressly said it was fair to describe the state confirmed on November 20 as a gap in these notes. If the feature ships, Crossmint will write on the thread and I can rerun the November 17 test.

## Cross-platform observations

These four tests do not support a ranking. They do suggest several distinctions worth preserving in future tests:

- **Enforcement is not evidence.** A control can stop the action correctly while leaving an incomplete owner-facing record.
- **A refusal can disappear before the main transaction object exists.** Catena's pre-intent cases and Locus's missing call-history entries are different implementations of a similar visibility problem.
- **Current settings are not historical evidence.** A useful record identifies the limit or policy version that applied at the time.
- **The latest checkout state is not the approval history.** Where a purchase can change, the record should preserve what the buyer approved at each relevant revision.
- **Per-user records are not automatically project-wide visibility.** An owner needs a practical way to discover and enumerate all relevant attempts.

A useful minimum owner-facing decision record would identify the attempted action, agent or end user, amount and currency, merchant or resource, time, outcome, reason code, and the control or approval state that applied. It should be available across the project, including refused attempts, without requiring the owner to reconstruct identifiers one at a time.

## Limitations

- Four platforms and a small scenario set are field evidence, not comprehensive coverage or an industry benchmark.
- Different products and environments were tested; results are not directly interchangeable.
- Tests covered selected external APIs and console views. They do not prove what internal logs contain.
- Retention, exports, every enterprise feature, and every pre-record validation failure were not exhaustively tested.
- Some test records came from saved API reads replayed into a local tool; their capture timestamps were not original event timestamps.
- Product behavior can change. Each result is dated, and vendor responses or fixes will produce dated updates rather than silent edits.
