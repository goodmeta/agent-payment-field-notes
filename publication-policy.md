# Publication, evidence and corrections policy

## Editorial scope

Good Meta field notes report reproducible observations, useful methods and representative evidence. They do not present small samples as rankings, comprehensive benchmarks or broad industry findings.

Reports include positive behavior and controls alongside gaps. There is no pay-to-play: payment, consulting relationships or commercial interest must not determine coverage, wording or findings. Any material conflict relevant to a note should be disclosed.

## Publication and right of reply

A note requires factual, source, link and scope review plus the author's final approval before publication.

Tested companies should receive a private heads-up and enough time for a substantive response before publication. This right of reply is an opportunity to identify errors and add context, not editorial approval or a veto.

Vendor responses are dated and summarized conservatively. Individuals are identified by role and company unless explicit naming approval exists. A statement that an improvement is planned or in progress is not reported as a working fix; changed behavior requires a dated retest.

## Evidence boundaries

Tests should record exact inputs, dates, environments, observed outputs and relevant controls. Raw evidence is preserved privately where needed for verification, with credentials and secrets excluded.

Public notes contain only the evidence needed to understand and check a finding. They do not include raw census archives, raw vendor evidence or private correspondence, endpoint-level historical datasets, blockchain joins, credentials, private user or account identifiers, or unannounced product roadmaps.

Public reproduction projects may be linked as external sources. Their contents and licenses remain those of the linked projects.

## Corrections and updates

After publication, correction requests can be submitted through a GitHub issue. Include the note and version, the scenario and date, the disputed statement, and supporting material that is safe to make public. Do not attach credentials, private correspondence or sensitive records.

Good Meta will assess the evidence and distinguish:

- **Corrections**, which fix an error in the original report; and
- **Updates**, which record a response, product change or later retest without implying that the original dated observation was wrong.

Material corrections receive a dated note identifying what changed. Substantive updates preserve the original observation and add the new test date and environment. Supported findings are not silently edited away. Routine typographical and formatting fixes need no correction entry.

A vendor disagreement alone does not invalidate a supported observation, and a vendor assurance alone does not establish changed behavior. When practical, the same scenarios and controls are rerun before a gap is marked resolved.
